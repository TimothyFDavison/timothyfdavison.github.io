# Conference Publications

I have authored or co-authored several (20+) research papers, technical reports, technical whitepapers, proposals,
memos, and other analytical products. However, the majority of these are not shareable publicly. 
Listed on this page are a few conference papers and talks which I *can* share and discuss.

My research interests have historically included generative multimedia and associated model architectures (especially 
generative adversarial networks and transformer attention mechanisms), computational social science, social 
network analysis, and code stylometry.

### Publications & Presentations

*Much of my work explores the generation, detection, and counter-detection of generative multimedia. In this paper, 
I collaborated with researchers at SUNY Buffalo including Chengzhe Sun and Siwei Lyu on detecting synthetic audio. 
We introduced a novel dataset and explored vocoder-based detection techniques.*

- Sun, C., Albadawy, E. A., __Davison, T.__, et al. (November 2023). *Using vocoder artifacts for audio 
deepfake detection.* In Adversarial Multimedia Forensics (pp. 263–282).

*In 2022, I served as a Technical Director within the CAE-R research program. In that capacity I posed technical 
challenges and advised on university-led research projects. My participation in the program culminated in being a 
panelist representing the program at the 2022 CAE-R symposium.*

- __Davison, T.__ (June 2022). *Technical Director Panel.* Panelist at the Centers of Academic Excellence in 
Cybersecurity Research (CAE-R) Symposium, Atlanta, GA.

*From 2019 through 2021 I served as the technical lead for code stylometry on a research grant funded by the Office of 
Naval Research (ONR) Minerva program. My team and I developed novel approaches to identify malware authors and conduct
Advanced Persistent Threat (APT) attribution of malware binaries.*

*Our early work replicated studies in this space. I built tools to featurize abstract syntax trees, decompiled code, 
strings, and information entropy of a given malware binary, then trained machine learning models 
to explore the authorship attribution potential of those features.*

*Our later work joined those features with social and network characteristics of malware authors. My
colleagues proposed a Malware Development Life Cycle as a complement to the traditional Software Development Life 
Cycle, and we integrated features from my code stylometry research within that framework.*

- Elder, M., __Davison, T.__, et al. (January 2021). *Network analysis of malware stylometry features for 
attribution.* Talk presented at the North American Social Networks (NASN) Conference, virtual.


- La Cholter, W., __Davison, T.__, et al. (December 2020). *GitHub experimentation framework for attribution.* 
Talk presented at the Annual Computer Security Applications Conference (ACSAC), Austin, TX.


- Cruickshank, I., Johnson, A., __Davison, T.__, et al. (January 2020). *Detecting malware communities using 
socio-cultural cognitive mapping.* Computational and Mathematical Organization Theory, 26, 307–319. 
https://doi.org/10.1007/s10588-019-09300-w


- __Davison, T.__ (July 2019). *Malware attribution using binary stylometry.* Talk presented at the Malware 
Technical Exchange Meeting (MTEM) 2019 Conference, El Segundo, CA.
